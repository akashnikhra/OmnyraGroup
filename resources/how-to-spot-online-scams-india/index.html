How to Spot Online Scams in India: 3 Traps and the Rules That Beat Them

By the OMNyra Group team. Published 22 November 2026

Most online scams in India rely on one of three psychological traps: giving you something first so you trust them (task scams), scaring you so you stop thinking (fake "digital arrest" calls), or offering two choices that both lead to handing over your OTP. Each has a simple rule that beats it: real jobs never ask you to pay, police never arrest anyone over a video call, and your OTP is never shared with anyone. If you have lost money, call 1930 immediately or report at cybercrime.gov.in.

Trap 1: they give first, so you stop doubting (task scams)

How it shows up: a message offers easy money for liking three videos or rating products. You do it and ₹150 really arrives. Next comes a "VIP task" that needs a ₹5,000 deposit. That money never comes back.

Why it works: reciprocity. A small, real payment removes suspicion and makes the next request feel like part of the deal.

The rule: a job that asks you for money is not a job.

Trap 2: they find your fear (digital arrest calls)

How it shows up: a video call from someone dressed as a police or CBI officer says a parcel in your name contains drugs. You are told you are under "digital arrest" and must not tell anyone while you "clear your name" by transferring money.

Why it works: fear plus isolation. Panic stops careful thinking, and secrecy stops family from warning you.

The rule: CBI, ED and the police do not arrest anyone over a video call. Ever. Hang up and tell your family.

Trap 3: they give you a choice where both options are theirs (OTP scams)

How it shows up: a caller says an order, subscription or account change is pending. "Want to cancel it? Tell me the OTP. Want to confirm it? Tell me the OTP."

Why it works: a false choice. You focus on which option to pick and miss that both hand over control.

The rule: your OTP stays with you. No bank, delivery company or government office needs it from you on a call.

How can I spot a scam in one second?

Ask three questions. Did they give me something first and now want money? Are they scaring me and telling me to keep it secret? Are they offering two options that both need my OTP or payment? If the answer to any is yes, stop.

What should I do if I have been scammed?

  • Call the national cyber crime helpline 1930 as soon as possible; speed improves the chance of freezing the money.
  • Report online at cybercrime.gov.in.
  • Call your bank to block cards and UPI, and change passwords for affected accounts.
  • Keep screenshots of messages, numbers and transaction IDs as evidence.
